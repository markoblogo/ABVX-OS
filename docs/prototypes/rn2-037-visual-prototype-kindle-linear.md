## Ohm’s law without the fog

One relationship connects three quantities:

- **Voltage (E), measured in volts:** the electrical potential difference—the available push between two points.
- **Current (I), measured in amperes:** the rate of charge flow through a path.
- **Resistance (R), measured in ohms:** opposition to current.

More push produces more flow. More opposition produces less flow when voltage stays fixed. These ideas support T5A05, T5A11, and T5D01–T5D12.

## Choose the formula from what is missing

- Need current in amperes? **I = E ÷ R.**
- Need voltage in volts? **E = I × R.**
- Need resistance in ohms? **R = E ÷ I.**

The familiar E-over-I-R triangle is only a memory aid. The three formulas describe the same relationship.

### Worked pattern: resistance

Given 12 volts and 4 amperes, the missing unit is ohms. Therefore use resistance equals voltage divided by current: 12 ÷ 4 = **3 ohms**. Pool links: T5D03 and T5D06.

### Worked pattern: current

Given 120 volts and 80 ohms, the missing unit is amperes. Therefore use current equals voltage divided by resistance: 120 ÷ 80 = **1.5 amperes**. Pool links: T5D01 and T5D07.

### Worked pattern: voltage

Given 2 amperes and 10 ohms, the missing unit is volts. Therefore use voltage equals current multiplied by resistance: 2 × 10 = **20 volts**. Pool links: T5D02 and T5D12.

## Series and parallel recognition

**Series means one path.** When charge has only one route, every component in that route carries the same current. Pool link: T5D13.

**Parallel means shared endpoints.** When branches connect to the same two points, each branch has the same voltage across it. Pool link: T5D14.

Do not classify a circuit by whether it is drawn horizontally or vertically. Trace the route: one uninterrupted path means series; a choice between branches means parallel.

## Meters follow the quantity

A voltmeter measures the difference between two points, so connect it across a component—in parallel. An ammeter measures flow through a path, so insert it into that path—in series.

Memory line: **Voltage looks across. Current goes through.** Pool links: T7D02–T7D03.

## Sixty-second self-check

1. A 12-volt source drives 4 amperes. What is the resistance?
2. A 10-ohm resistor carries 2 amperes. What voltage is across it?
3. In a series circuit, which quantity is the same through every component?
4. In parallel branches, which quantity is the same across each branch?
5. Does a voltmeter go in series or in parallel?

Answers: **3 ohms; 20 volts; current; voltage; parallel.**
