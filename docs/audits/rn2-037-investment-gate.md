## Product gap and thesis

Current offers cluster around three promises: complete 409-question banks, prose manuals, and app/video practice. “Plain-English explanations,” practice exams, memory hooks, and audio are already saturated claims.

The authorized thesis is narrower and materially different:

> **A diagram-first concept map for nontechnical beginners: learn the small set of visual models and decision rules behind the Technician pool, then use a question-ID crosswalk to practise elsewhere.**

Working title: **Ham Radio Technician Visual Cram Map 2026–2030**  
Subtitle: **The Diagram-First Guide to the FCC Element 2 Concepts, Rules, and Calculations Beginners Mix Up**

It will not re-explain all 409 questions sequentially. It will use approximately 60–80 original diagrams, flowcharts, comparison panels, formula pictures, band/privilege maps, safety decision paths, and “looks similar but is different” pages. A complete question-ID crosswalk shows where each pool item is covered. Six diagnostic mini-tests target misconceptions; full randomized exams remain better served by free software.

Why this beats alternatives:

- shorter and more visual than the 208–256-page institutional manuals;
- teaches relationships that flashcard apps intentionally reduce to isolated prompts;
- works as a physical desk reference beside free HamStudy-style practice;
- avoids competing on “more questions,” generic explanations, or pass guarantees;
- the exact “visual/illustrated” Amazon query did not surface a current title explicitly owning diagram-first positioning on its first page.

Buyer evidence is directional rather than conclusive: current learner discussions repeatedly distinguish rote app study from needing concepts or visuals, while recommending video when books feel too textual. The product converts that need into a fast physical reference. This is the principal investment uncertainty.

## Autonomous QA

1. Freeze the February 2026 NCVEC pool and diagrams; store URL, retrieval time, hash, effective dates, errata, and rights statement.

3. Map every manuscript fact, formula, diagram, and question-ID crosswalk row to sources in a claim ledger.
4. Run deterministic checks: all 409 IDs present once in the crosswalk; correct-answer keys match; errata incorporated; withdrawn IDs absent; formulas unit-tested; frequency/band data checked against current Part 97.
5. Have a separate audit pass regenerate answers/explanations from sources without consulting draft prose, then compare conclusions.
6. Render and visually inspect every diagram at print size and Kindle width. Verify accessibility text.
7. Repeat NCVEC/FCC refresh at proof lock and upload day. Any source drift blocks release.
