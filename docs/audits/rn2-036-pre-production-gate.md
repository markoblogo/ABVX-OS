## Currentness and controlling facts

The **2025 Naturalization Civics Test** is USCIS's reimplementation of the 2020 test with modifications. A Form N-400 filed **before 20 October 2025** uses the 2008 test; one filed **on or after 20 October 2025** uses the 2025 test. Both therefore remain relevant in 2026.

For the ordinary 2025 civics test, an officer asks orally **up to 20** questions from a **128-question** bank. Testing stops at **12 correct** or **9 incorrect** answers; 12 correct passes.

For **65/20 special consideration**—age 65 or older and at least 20 years as a lawful permanent resident—the applicant studies 20 starred questions, may test in a language of choice, is asked up to 10, and passes with 6 correct. Filing date still determines the test version.

The location- or officeholder-dependent answers are Q23 (senator), Q29 (representative), Q30 (Speaker), Q38 (President), Q39 (Vice President), Q57 (Chief Justice), and Q61 (governor). Q62 (state capital) varies by location but is not normally election-volatile. USCIS requires the official serving at interview time.

### Volatility / refresh ledger

- **Filing-date routing; 128/up-to-20/12/9 rules; 65/20 rules:** medium volatility. Verify at source lock and immediately before publication.
- **Q23, Q29, Q61:** high and applicant-specific. Never print one universal answer; link to official Senate, House, and USAGov lookups and tell readers to refresh near interview.
- **Q30, Q38, Q39, Q57:** high. Pull accepted answers from USCIS at source lock, proof lock, and the day of upload; record retrieval date and page hash.
- **Q62:** low but applicant-specific; use the official state/territory lookup.
- **All other accepted answers:** low-to-medium. Mechanically compare the complete answer bank with the current official PDF before publication.

## Rights and source use

17 U.S.C. §§101 and 105 define a U.S. government work and generally deny U.S. copyright protection to work prepared by a federal employee as part of official duties. This supports reproducing USCIS-authored official question wording and accepted-answer text with attribution. It is not a blanket license for every asset in every government PDF.

- **Official USCIS questions/accepted answers:** reproduce exactly after verifying government authorship; label and attribute.
- **Government explanations:** paraphrase and cite, even where reusable, for originality and maintainability.
- **Layouts, photos, illustrations, maps, audio, and embedded assets:** exclude absent item-level rights verification; government publications can contain licensed third-party components.
- **Third-party books/sites/apps/translations/mnemonics:** market evidence only; do not copy expression.
- **USCIS/DHS visual identity:** do not use seals, logos, signatures, or imitations. Descriptive references must not imply affiliation.

Required notice: “Independent study aid. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or published by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services or the U.S. government. Check official questions and accepted answers at uscis.gov before your interview.” Add dated source attribution. This is a production policy, not legal advice.

Rights sources: [17 U.S.C. §105](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#105), [USCIS trademark/logo terms](https://myaccount.uscis.gov/legal-terms), and [18 U.S.C. §701](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/701).

## Product gap and competition

The strongest plausible thesis was: **a compact, large-type, simple-English oral-practice book that first routes readers to the correct test, then trains concise spoken answers and makes volatile answers easy to refresh.** It excludes a full N-400 guide, immigration advice, and multilingual expansion.

Buyers plausibly pay for physical sequencing, annotation space, simplified context, oral drills, and a last-week plan. Demand remains real. A refreshed Amazon query showed **179 results** and multiple 2025–2026 entrants with visible ratings. A December 2025 entrant showed 71 ratings; other recent entrants showed 35, 44, and 82. Prices displayed in the France-localized session were roughly EUR 10–25. These are volatile observations, not sales data.

The gap is not clearly underserved. Listings already promise easy answers, all 128 questions, interview prep, practice tests, audio, flash cards, large print, and language editions. One June 2026 listing showed more than 1,100 ratings. Sponsored results indicate that paid visibility participates in the market.

- **Versus free USCIS:** sequencing and paper practice help, but USCIS is authoritative and free.
- **Versus free web/apps:** offline and writable use help, but apps handle audio, repetition, location-specific answers, and updates better.
- **Versus Amazon books:** the exact proposed thesis is already represented.

This is demand without a defensible new product hypothesis. RN2-036 would test execution and packaging in a crowded thesis, not a meaningfully different buyer job or format. That fails the user's hard gate.

2. Draft only from approved ledger items; tag every official question/answer by source and number.
3. Map every rule, threshold, exception, process statement, date, and current answer in a claim ledger.

5. Refresh Q23, Q29, Q30, Q38, Q39, Q57, Q61, Q62 at proof lock.
