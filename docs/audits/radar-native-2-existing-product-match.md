# RN2 Existing Product Match Audit

**Decision date:** 5 September 2026  
**Decision:** RN2-001 is an **EXISTING_PRODUCT_MATCH** to the same market thesis as *The Human Backup Plan*. It is not approved for production. RN2-036 becomes the RN2 selection, also without production authorization.

## Direct comparison

RN2-001 targets households that want one place to record critical information before an emergency. Its job is to make important information findable and actionable by another person. The proposed product is a compact, substantive fill-in workbook reached through queries such as `family emergency binder` and `in case of emergency organizer`.

*The Human Backup Plan* targets a modern household operator, small-business owner, creator, or family member who needs someone else to keep life running during incapacity, absence, or death. Its job is the same handoff: map what exists, show another person where to begin, and define what to keep, transfer, cancel, archive, or delete. The trigger and promise are therefore substantially the same. Its scope is wider: money, devices, documents, digital assets, domains, royalties, crypto, subscriptions, AI systems, business projects, secure recovery, and 24-hour/7-day/30-day continuity.

The meaningful differences are product surface and search language. RN2-001 is a simple household organizer and writing tool. *The Human Backup Plan* is a 251-page operating-manual-style book with Kindle and paperback editions. RN2-001 speaks the established `family emergency binder` language; the existing book leads with `continuity`, `digital assets`, and `business`, and explicitly rejects the binder category.

## Similarity score

The regression fixture applies fixed weights to nine facets:

- Buyer 80; JTBD 95; trigger 90; promise 92; audience 82.
- Format 55; information architecture 78; search intent 62; differentiation 40.
- Weighted semantic/product-market overlap: **82.9/100**.
- Classification: **EXISTING_PRODUCT_MATCH**.
- Portfolio Similarity Gate: **blocked** for a separate RN2-001 book.

The score is a structured judgment, not a machine-derived semantic measurement. The high overlap comes from the shared job, trigger, and outcome; the lower format and search scores explain why the existing product may not capture the observed binder demand.

## Current Amazon packaging versus RN2 evidence

Amazon currently shows *The Human Backup Plan* as a Kindle edition at USD 4.99 (also in Kindle Unlimited) and a paperback displayed at EUR 13.79 for the France-localized session. The paperback is 251 pages, 6 × 9 inches, independently published on 19 August 2026. Both format pages showed zero customer reviews on 5 September 2026. The Kindle breadcrumb placed it under Business & Money / Information Management, while the paperback breadcrumb placed it under Self-Help / Relationships / Love & Loss. That split reinforces the product's broad, difficult-to-place promise. [Amazon Kindle listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HFTTG5FH), [Amazon paperback listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HFTT2K3S).

RN2-001 won partly because the narrower query returned approximately 739 results and a recently published 107-page *Family Emergency Handbook* had 44 ratings and a visible Books rank of about 123,801 in the original snapshot. Its title directly promises a preparedness planner for vital information, finances, insurance, passwords, and documents. This is evidence for the category and format, not proof that every competing binder sells.

The comparison produces four directional findings:

1. **Paperback/workbook over Kindle:** supported directionally. The winning market example is a writable 107-page planner; *Human Backup Plan* is available in paperback but presents as a long reading product. Format-level sales data are unavailable, so preference strength is not proven.
2. **Fill-in organizer over prose guide:** supported. The observed newcomer traction belongs to an explicit planner, while the existing 251-page book has no visible review traction after roughly seventeen days. That is too early to call commercial failure, but it does not rebut the format signal.
3. **Family/emergency language over continuity/digital-assets language:** strongly supported for search interception. `Family emergency binder` names the buyer's familiar category. The existing title requires the shopper to understand a new abstraction.
4. **Simpler household scope over business/digital complexity:** likely. The existing promise spans family, money, business, crypto, AI, domains, and projects. This increases value for complex households but weakens instant category recognition. Amazon's inconsistent category placement is consistent with that problem, though it does not prove causation.

## Repositioning conclusion

Do not create a substantially duplicate RN2-001 book. The existing product can test the thesis by changing its packaging and usable form later, after a separate approval:

- retain *The Human Backup Plan* as the canonical product;
- lead the description and subtitle more clearly with family emergency handoff and incapacity readiness;
- expose the workbook/checklist nature on the cover and sample, or revise the existing paperback into a more writable hybrid edition;
- simplify the front-of-book path for ordinary households while preserving advanced business/digital sections as an optional layer;
- align categories and keywords around household organization, emergency preparedness, and family records where KDP rules permit.

This would be a **reposition/reformat of an existing thesis**, not authorization for a new book. No listing or manuscript was changed during this audit.

## Replacement RN2 selection

**RN2-036 — 2025 U.S. Citizenship Civics Test Guide** is the strongest genuinely novel remaining opportunity. It has the highest stored RN2 score (89.4), visible newcomer evidence in the original Amazon scan, an estimated 4–6 Anton-hours, and an official current source baseline. USCIS confirms that the 2025 test uses 128 possible questions, asks up to 20 orally, and requires 12 correct answers; some answers change with elections or appointments and must be refreshed. [USCIS official 128 questions](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/questions-and-answers/2025-Civics-Test-128-Questions-and-Answers.pdf).

RN2-037 remains reserve rather than winner. Its 2026–2030 Technician pool is current and explicitly public domain, but the market includes authoritative ARRL materials, established specialist competitors, and free online preparation. [NCVEC question pool](https://ncvec.org/index.php/2026-2030-technician-question-pool), [ARRL resources](https://www.arrl.org/instruction-arrl-resources).

RN2-036 selection is **not production approval**. Before production it still needs its own portfolio-match check, exact filing-date/test-version routing, current-officeholder update mechanism, public-domain and government-affiliation language review, and a final ≤6-hour feasibility gate.

## Evidence limits

Amazon ranks, prices, reviews, categories, and result counts are volatile snapshots. No KDP sales dashboard data were used. Zero reviews do not mean zero sales. The research stopped after the exact existing product, the original RN2 comparison product, and the two remaining top candidates provided enough evidence to decide the similarity gate and replacement ranking.
