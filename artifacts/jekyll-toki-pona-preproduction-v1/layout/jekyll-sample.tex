\layer{ENGLISH SOURCE — chapter II, abridged only for layout testing}
That evening Mr. Utterson opened his safe and took out the will of Dr. Jekyll. It provided that Jekyll’s possessions should pass to Edward Hyde if Jekyll died, disappeared, or was absent without explanation for more than three months. The lawyer had long hated the document. Now that he knew something of Hyde, the name began to gather detestable attributes.

\layer{ENGLISH SOURCE — chapter VI, abridged only for layout testing}
Poole led Utterson through the old dissecting rooms to Jekyll’s cabinet. Jekyll looked deathly ill. He said he would never see Hyde again, but asked Utterson whether a letter should go to the police. The letter, signed Edward Hyde, promised that Hyde had a means of escape. Jekyll had burned the envelope; Poole had seen no messenger.
