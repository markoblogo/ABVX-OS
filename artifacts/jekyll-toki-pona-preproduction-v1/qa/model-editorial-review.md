# Model editorial review — representative Toki Pona sample

**Scope:** source sample / model-draft Toki Pona sample only.  
**Reviewer type:** model editorial QA; this is not evidence of human linguistic or literary approval.  
**Review question:** does the rendering preserve plot-relevant meaning, uncertainty, character knowledge, and readable Toki Pona without importing the final revelation?

## Editorial finding

The sample is **workable as a draft, not publishable as a translation**. Its principal weakness is not vocabulary scarcity. It is that several compact formulations turn a claim, fear, legal condition, or private inference into a simpler fact. Narrative fiction needs an explicit editorial pass at those knowledge boundaries.

## Paragraph-level editorial notes

### A. Will and Lanyon

The draft successfully keeps the will, Hyde, Lanyon, and Utterson distinct and does not disclose the identity relation. It loses legal conditionality, the contrast between name and reputation, and Lanyon's very particular professional contempt. The correct target is not Victorian sentence length; it is a clear sequence of **document says → condition → Utterson judges → Lanyon knows only part of the story**.

### B. Letter and laboratory

The draft retains the letter as an uncertain object rather than proof, which is good. It needs tighter control of who knows what. Jekyll's certainty must remain unexplained; Utterson's fear of a trial remains a possibility, not a predicted outcome; the missing messenger remains a new reason for suspicion. The current `mi pilin e sina` does not carry “I have great trust in you.”

### C. Poole

The draft captures urgency and refusal to explain, but it is too compressed to preserve Poole's embarrassed, dogged fear. This is an appropriate place to permit a slightly longer Toki Pona turn rather than deleting the character rhythm.

## Narrative controls required in the full manuscript

1. **Knowledge tags in the alignment data:** narrator fact, Utterson inference, Lanyon opinion, Poole testimony, letter claim, and later confession cannot be merged by a translator.
2. **Section-aware spoiler controls:** a guide must never define Jekyll/Hyde, explain the draught, or turn a later document into early navigation help.
3. **Controlled legal and medical phrasebook:** the translator needs source-context notes, not one-word glosses.
4. **Name registry before translation:** transliteration, Latin fallback/cartouche form, case treatment, and visual width must be locked before the first batch.
5. **Dialogue QA:** preserve speaker, question, hesitation, denial, and uncertainty. Compression may remove ornament, not speech-act logic.

## What a human Toki Pona reviewer must decide

- whether the phonetic name forms are natural and recognisable;
- whether `jan`, role phrases, and repeated descriptions remain unambiguous in dialogue;
- whether reduced Victorian moral/scientific language preserves tone without importing a modern diagnosis;
- whether each identity-sensitive passage preserves the reader's knowledge at that exact section;
- whether sitelen pona remains readable rather than an attractive but slower duplicate layer.

## Editorial verdict

**MODEL QA: REWRITE REQUIRED.** The issues are identifiable and bounded, which supports narrative feasibility. They do not establish literary quality and do not replace the separate KDP policy/disclosure refresh required immediately before release.
