# Cross-Workstream Findings

This note compares structure, not sales causality. It does not propose Radar changes.

## What the Toki Pona system appears to have

| Evidence-supported factor | Toki Pona system | Radar-native cohort |
|---|---|---|
| Audience identity | language/script/conlang reader is explicit | PMP/Ham are broad exam candidates; French is a defined bilingual transition; Solar buyer may not be a book buyer |
| Query clarity | `source + toki pona` names a singular object | PMP/Ham queries resolve to high-volume exam-prep promises; Solar phrase does not resolve to a peer book shelf |
| Product legibility | famous source + unusual language + sitelen reads as one distinct object | orientation versus exam prep and visual cram map require more explanation; Solar's unique value lacks a shelf context |
| Amazon-native intent | small, direct book query observed for Odyssey | French has one direct bilingual book query; Solar did not; PMP/Ham are crowded shelves |
| Competition | direct TP-source query was short | PMP/Ham have dense competitors and ads; French competes indirectly with French test prep |
| Series/system | visible series link and existing catalogue | cohort products are mostly standalone |
| Reuse | parallel translation/layout pipeline is reusable | source-dependent pipelines are less reusable or require wider subject expertise |
| Differentiation | transformation is visible in title | product benefit is often invisible until description/detail page |

## Implications to hold for later Radar discussion

1. Direct Amazon-query observability is a missing measurement layer; do not equate exact-title indexing with buyer discovery.
2. A coherent object query and existing collection may matter as much as a plausible problem, but current evidence cannot quantify either effect.
3. Solar is the clearest caution: a serious buyer problem can still be weak Amazon-book intent.
4. These are hypotheses for a future Radar v2 review, not justification to redesign scoring or to alter listings now.
