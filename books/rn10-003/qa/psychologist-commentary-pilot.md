# Psychologist-commentary pilot after Act I

## Candidate interlude — not inserted

> **Marginal note attached to the South Vesper incident file; author unknown**
>
> Relief is easy to mistake for repair because both can make a person stand up in the morning. The difference appears later, in what the person is able to refuse.
>
> Pain can imprison, but it also reports the shape of an injury. Fear can become a cage, but it can also mark the door through which danger entered. Remove either without changing its cause and the wounded person may become wonderfully efficient at returning to the same fire.
>
> This is why care so often becomes control while still smelling like love. The caretaker sees suffering, discovers a way to quiet it, and begins protecting the remedy from the person it serves. The secret seems merciful because disagreement would reintroduce pain. Soon the absence of disagreement becomes evidence that the remedy works.
>
> A safer form of care leaves room for an inconvenient answer. It permits anger, delay, refusal, and departure. It may look less kind from outside because the suffering remains visible.
>
> The dog understood part of this only after she had already used trust as a leash. Understanding did not undo the act. It merely returned the next choice to her.

## Comparison

The clean transition ends with the shepherd ordering Mriya to choose Good Dog or Bad Dog while she keeps walking. Chapter 10 immediately tests that demand. The interlude pauses the confrontation, explains the act's central metaphor and introduces an archival observer before the story has established a record or psychologist.

## Decision

**REMOVE.** The prose is thematically compatible but performs interpretation the scenes already earned. It weakens urgency and narrows reader judgment. No version of this note enters the manuscript, and no recurring psychologist-commentary system will be used in volume 1.
