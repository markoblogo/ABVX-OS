## Expansion method

For each chapter, add complete scene units in this order:

1. Entry pressure and immediate objective.
2. Obstacle or competing interpretation.
3. Reversal caused by a character choice.
4. Consequence that persists into the next chapter.
5. Only then add sensory texture, dialogue and humor.

Do not distribute the word gap evenly. Use the exact chapter allocations in the expansion map. A chapter may finish within 10% of its target, but each act must finish within 3% before the next act is expanded.

## Optional psychologist-commentary device

The owner-supplied psychology notes contain strong thematic matches for this novel: false selves, emotional numbing, care whose main word is no, boundaries that also permit yes, dependence as escape, borrowed identity, and past pain dictating present choice.

**Decision: pilot, not adopted.** Commentary after every chapter would break close-third dog POV, repeat themes the scenes already express and slow LitRPG progression. After E1, create one 180–250-word in-world psychologist's marginal note after chapter 9 and compare the transition with the clean version.

Adopt the device only if it adds a new inference, avoids diagnosing the characters, preserves the act-ending impulse and does not imply clinical authority. If adopted, use at most one note per act and deduct its words from that act's existing target. Preserve chapter 36's final line as the final narrative line.

Potential thematic mappings, expressed as adaptations rather than quotations:

- Act I: numbing fear also numbs joy; support can help change or merely make stasis tolerable.
- Act II: care requires the capacity to say no, while healed boundaries recover the capacity to say yes.
- Act III: dependence promises escape while narrowing the ability to leave.
- Act IV: a borrowed perfect identity cannot replace a self that keeps choosing in the present.

Do not reuse client anecdotes, diagnostic claims, third-party quotations embedded in the notes or identifiable clinical material.
