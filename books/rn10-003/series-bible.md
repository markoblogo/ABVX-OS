# Good Dogs of the Apocalypse — series bible

## Narrative contract

The series begins as an affectionate dog-and-owner story and becomes a black comedy about civilization, dependency and power. The dogs never stop being lovable. That is why their rise is funny, moving and frightening.

Book 1 uses close third person through Mriya. Her reasoning is intelligent but embodied: scent precedes sight, people are recognized by emotional weather and routine, words matter less than breath, posture and the hand that reaches toward a leash. The System gives labels to things she already understands; it does not turn her into a small human in fur.

## Setting

Vesper is a fictional contemporary European port city built over service tunnels, culverts, abandoned rail spurs and older foundations. Its public surface is orderly enough to make the hidden infrastructure feel intimate rather than post-nuclear. The System arrives locally and quietly before its citywide scale becomes visible.

Technology continues to work at first. Social systems fail sooner than machines because the System changes incentives: dogs can see Caches and Territory, while humans can only see the consequences.

## Principal characters

### Mriya

An aging mixed-breed shelter dog with a damaged left ear, powerful scent memory and little faith in permanent homes. She wants safety, food and Nika within smelling distance. Her flaw is protective possession: she can confuse keeping someone safe with deciding for them. Her rare Stray class rewards loyalty that is chosen rather than commanded.

### Nika Vale

A thirty-four-year-old courier whose life has contracted to routes, bills and exhausted competence. She adopts Mriya partly because neither of them can bear another temporary arrangement. Nika is practical, affectionate and resistant to mystical explanations. Her need for relief makes the System’s helpful bargains dangerous.

### Borscht

A small, round, socially fearless dog who treats every closed door as a clerical error. He supplies warmth and comic momentum, but his instinct for pleasing humans makes him vulnerable to the Good Dog path.

### Atlas

A retired police shepherd whose training gives him status among dogs and rigidity under uncertainty. He believes rules keep packs alive. Mriya’s Stray class threatens his moral map before it earns his respect.

### Pixel

A young terrier who reads the city through vibration, machinery and gaps no larger dog notices. She wants levels faster than wisdom and sees Caches as a game until the costs become personal.

### Rook

A Doberman enforcer serving the first organized rival pack. He values tools, competence and legible hierarchy. He enters as a threat and becomes a conditional ally when Mriya builds something worth respecting.

### Tomas, Irena and Vale

Three human followers delivered to the Pack Hearth by a System reward. Tomas offers practical repair skills, Irena understands municipal systems, and Vale is a charming opportunist. Their incompatible needs turn “human resources” into people Mriya must choose either to command or include.

### Custodian 0.03

A miniature white-clad administrator who treats catastrophe as maintenance. Its scale is absurd; its authority is not. It harvests System resources from insects and grants provisional charters to settlements that demonstrate stable dependency.

## System rules

Only dogs initially see the interface. The System recognizes intentional concealment as a Cache, including objects, routes, information and eventually motives. Finding is not ownership; the claiming choice determines rewards and consequences.

Primary stats are Scent, Bond, Pack, Territory and Adaptation. Resources are Trace, Favor, Supply and Territory. Numbers stay sparse. A stat increase must unlock a perception, relationship, coordinated action or physical change.

Good Dog grants power through obedience and human approval. Bad Dog grants power through fear and violation. Stray grants power through negotiated loyalty, mixed groups and solutions outside the offered binary. Stray is harder to level because consent cannot be farmed as efficiently as obedience or fear.

Facilities are social machines, not decorative bases. A Pack Hearth provides rest only if its inhabitants accept shared rules. A Scent Archive preserves discoveries but also makes private histories searchable. A Treat Exchange stabilizes supply while turning affection into currency. Every useful facility creates a new ethical problem.

## Style and tone

- Warm sensory specificity before lore explanation.
- Dry comedy from mismatched dog and human assumptions.
- System notices are short, rare and consequential.
- Violence is sharp and readable, never the main pleasure.
- No constant quipping, meme chains or ironic distance from emotion.
- End chapters on a changed choice, relationship or rule rather than an arbitrary interruption.

## Book-level arc

Book 1: one home becomes the first Pack Hearth.

Book 2: the Hearth becomes a district with trades, classes and rival institutions.

Book 3: the district becomes a government and forces Mriya to choose between stewardship and Plan Barbossa.

The larger series may later organize around instinct, territory, pack, mortality, command and self, but only the first three books are contracted.
