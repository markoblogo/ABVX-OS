# Voice contract — Good Dog, Bad System

## Viewpoint

Close third person through Mriya. Stay inside what she can smell, hear, feel, remember or infer. Visual description is allowed but scent and bodily orientation lead. Human dialogue can be understood normally; Mriya’s interpretation should still notice tone and body before abstract intent.

## Sentence behavior

Use clean, concrete prose with occasional long sensory sentences when several smells overlap. Action shortens the syntax. Emotional scenes become quieter, not more ornate. Avoid generic fantasy intensifiers, stacked adjectives and repeated explanation after the reader already understands.

## Humor

Humor comes from sincere priorities: a municipal boundary and a lamppost can describe the same political fact to different species. Dogs do not deliver stand-up routines. The narrator may be dry, but never mocks Mriya’s attachment to Nika.

## System text

System notices use blockquotes and brackets in the working manuscript. They must remain legible when converted to reflowable Kindle text. Maximum one compact notice per meaningful turn unless interface overload is itself the scene conflict.

## Cozy-to-dark control

The opening must work as a genuine story of two tired creatures making a home. Dark elements first appear as minor distortions of care: a reward for eye contact, a measurable Bond, a treat that changes mood. The transition should feel inevitable in retrospect, not like a genre replacement.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- No exposition dump explaining the whole System.
- No dog thoughts written as baby talk.
- No copied phrasing or close recreation from the situation bank.
- No invincible protagonist, unexplained skill or consequence-free upgrade.
- No repeated “she realized,” “she knew,” or summary of the previous scene.
- No promises in the prose that exist only to satisfy metadata.
