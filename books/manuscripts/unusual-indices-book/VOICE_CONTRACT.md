# Voice Contract — Burgers, Lipstick & Underwear

Status: `READY_FOR_HUMAN_APPROVAL`

## Target voice

Smart popular nonfiction: dry, skeptical, fast, clear and occasionally provocative.

The prose should feel like a sharp observer explaining why serious people keep finding meaning in ridiculous-looking measures.

## Delivery

- Use Jeremy Clarkson-style directness and comic contrast without imitating cars, catchphrases or persona.
- Let humor come from observation, proportion, absurdity and exact phrasing.
- Prefer short, forceful explanations after concrete scenes.
- Treat the reader as intelligent, impatient and allergic to textbook padding.
- Explain technical ideas through objects, behavior and stakes.

## What to avoid

- Encyclopedia voice.
- Academic literature review.
- Corporate thought leadership.
- 1D3X / POP / SPIKE promotion.
- Formulaic AI transitions.
- Repetitive constructions:
  - “The catch is...”
  - “Here’s the thing...”
  - “What happens when...”
  - “Of course...”
  - “In today’s world...”

## Factual posture

- Strong claims need sources.
- Folklore must be labeled internally as folklore or contested.
- A memorable story is not automatically evidence.
- Do not upgrade a media-friendly anecdote into a verified economic law.
- When a measure is useful but limited, say so plainly.

## Rhythm

Chapters may use:

1. a concrete hook;
2. the strange measure;
3. what it claims to reveal;
4. why people remember it;
5. what the evidence actually supports;
6. the failure/myth/manipulation angle;
7. a broader idea;
8. a clean transition.

This is not a rigid template. Chapters should vary so the book does not feel machine-generated.

## Reader effect

After a good chapter, the reader should be able to tell someone else:

“Did you know people use X to measure Y — and the mad part is, it’s partly useful and partly nonsense?”
