# Bracken Ford Product Development Specification

**Experiment:** `FACTORY_ORIGINATED_PRODUCT_EXPERIMENT_01`  
**Derived from:** `BRACKEN_FORD_COMMERCIAL_AUDIT_V1.md`  
**Status:** specification only — no drafting, editing, packaging, or publication authorised

## Product thesis

Create **No Further Claims**, an interconnected darkly comic *Bracken Ford* novel-in-stories about how partial observations become public narratives, then institutional facts. The book is a complete adult-fiction experience with a town-scale consequence—not a bound collection of eight whimsical premises.

## Target reader and promise

**Reader:** an adult reader of dry, humane literary comedy and small-town satire who wants character consequence alongside absurdity.

**Promise:** In Bracken Ford, Pennsylvania, the wrong story does not disappear when somebody corrects it. It acquires witnesses, paperwork, and a permanent place in town.

## Genre, tone, and constraints

- Primary: darkly comic literary fiction / small-town satire.
- Secondary signal: interconnected town novel.
- Not: cozy mystery, animal comedy, inspirational fiction, magical realism, legal thriller, or a short-story anthology.
- Preserve: exact comic detail, restrained dialogue, practical cause-and-effect, compassion for people who are wrong, and the lived cost of a public error.
- Avoid: identical misunderstanding/reveal/aphorism endings; Mrs Bell as an all-purpose truth machine; characters who exist only to misunderstand; faux official documents as the dominant form; cute animal-led packaging.

## Recommended shape

**Public form:** “A Bracken Ford Novel.”  
**Internal form:** 12–15 linked chapters/stories.  
**Target range:** 35,000–50,000 words.  
**Anchor distribution:** 3–4 substantial 5,000–8,000-word anchors; 5–7 medium 2,500–5,000-word chapters; 2–4 short bridges only where they alter the common story.

The final narrative needs a present-day municipal/social event whose account passes through multiple institutions. Each chapter must change at least one of: what the town believes, who bears the cost, the evidence available, a character relationship, or the final event’s stakes.

## Required structural spine

1. **Opening hook:** use *The Bird in the Dog in the Report* early enough to sell the book’s comic town engine, while giving Eli and the event a consequence beyond the reveal.
2. **Circulation:** show an interpretation moving through homes, store, road, theatre, records, and municipal authority. Do not simply repeat variants.
3. **Pressure:** make the town’s preferred story create a present problem for Eli, Mercer, Mrs Bell, or another principal resident.
4. **Complication:** a correction must be incomplete, late, costly, or socially inconvenient.
5. **Final event:** *The Inspector’s Missing Bag* may close only if it resolves a book-scale question about record, responsibility, and personhood; otherwise design a new final story.
6. **Aftermath:** end with changed practice or changed relationships, not only a polished proverb.

## New material brief

Develop a maximum of five new story concepts before choosing which to draft:

1. **Current civic event (required):** a town matter with actual stakes—public safety, access, local history, infrastructure, or an annual event—where an early account gains authority before facts are settled. The story must distribute consequences among several current characters.
2. **Eli-centred choice (required):** adult Eli must decide whether preserving a clean record harms a person, or correcting it harms the town’s preferred identity. He cannot simply discover the answer.
3. **Mrs Bell-centred story (required):** give her an objective and a blind spot; she cannot only deliver the theme.
4. **Mercer or institutional counterpart:** make responsibility conflict with the need to act on incomplete information.
5. **Optional counter-rhythm story:** a relationship, labour, or family story in which the premise operates quietly rather than as a public comic spectacle.

No new story qualifies merely because it contains a wrong assumption. It must feed the shared event or deepen a principal character.

## World bible requirements

Before expansion, document:

- Bracken Ford geography and repeatable locations: store, road/route, library/archive, town hall, theatre, lodge/lake, law-enforcement presence, private homes.
- Chronology of the stroller/road incident, bird incident, Eli’s later archive role, and current final event.
- Character facts and relationships: Eli, Tom, Leah, Mrs Bell, Mercer, Hank, Baron, Gideon, Lena, Mara, Owen, and any retained newcomer.
- Every existing incident: observed fact; public claim; official record; what the reader knows; unresolved ambiguity; later consequence.
- Style rules for civic documents, signs, reports, and dialogue. Official material may appear sparingly but cannot replace scenes.

## Packaging direction after manuscript gate

Working public title: **No Further Claims: A Bracken Ford Novel**.

Alternative title route if the bird becomes the primary book engine: **The Giant Eagle of Bracken Ford** with a clear adult-fiction subtitle. Test both only after the structure is locked.

Cover should be spare and adult: a municipal evidence marker, redacted record, map/grid, or incident footprint; a small anomalous silhouette is acceptable. Avoid the “official minutes” and town-meeting-document visual vocabulary used by close marketplace competition.

## Amazon discovery hypothesis

The book will be discoverable through an adult comic-fiction premise and emerging Bracken Ford identity, not through “linked short stories.” Packaging language may test “darkly comic small-town novel,” “literary comedy,” and “small-town satire.” This remains a weak-to-plausible hypothesis that must be validated at the packaging gate, not treated as demand proof.

## Human and editorial requirements

This is **substantial human work**. The source prototype can guide development, but a fluent English-language fiction editor/human reviewer must independently assess structure, voice consistency, rhythm, character depth, continuity, and market clarity before publication.

Required non-writing checks:

1. Pen-name/title availability and confusion check.
2. Private provenance declaration/source ledger.
3. Similarity screening after the full manuscript is complete.
4. Privacy/defamation pass for invented town, characters, and incidents.
5. Target-reader feedback from adult comic/literary-fiction readers.

## Gates and stop conditions

- **Stop** if the developed outline cannot create one book-scale consequence without mechanically connecting the stories.
- **Stop** if close-comparable research finds the resulting title/promise materially duplicative of an existing current product.
- **Stop** if development relies on bulk-filling the word range with interchangeable misunderstanding sketches.
- **Stop** if provenance/originality cannot be documented satisfactorily.
- **Proceed to a manuscript-development approval gate only** when the structural spine, retained-story list, new-story briefs, and world bible are all accepted.

No KDP metadata, cover, layout, advertising, listing, or production work is authorised by this specification.
