## Frozen product

### Product concept

A black-and-white, diagram-first Technician-class exam companion for nontechnical first-time candidates using the United States FCC Element 2 pool effective 1 July 2026 through 30 June 2030. It teaches a limited set of visual models, contrasts, decision paths, and calculation patterns, then maps every official question ID to the concept that supports it. It complements free question practice; it does not reproduce the experience of a sequential question bank.

### Working title

**Ham Radio Technician Visual Cram Map 2026–2030**

### Subtitle direction

**A Diagram-First Guide to the FCC Element 2 Concepts, Rules, and Calculations Beginners Mix Up**

Title and subtitle remain working packaging, not a claim of FCC authorship. A normal metadata/trademark search is required before final packaging, but it is not a subject-matter gate.

### Buyer, situation, and job to be done

The buyer is a U.S. adult or older teen preparing for a first Technician-class amateur-radio examination who does not arrive with electronics or radio knowledge. They have seen the 409-question pool, tried an app, video, or conventional manual, and can recognize some answers but cannot keep the underlying relationships straight. Their immediate job is: **build enough mental structure to understand and rapidly retrieve the concepts most likely to be confused, while retaining a verifiable path to every official question ID.**

The trigger is an intended exam within roughly two to eight weeks, often after the learner discovers that memorizing isolated answers is brittle. The book lets this buyer move faster from “these are unrelated facts” to “I can see which model applies,” then return to a free app for randomized exam practice.

### Primary promise

**See the small set of patterns behind the 2026–2030 Technician pool, recognize which one a question is testing, and find every official question ID through a complete crosswalk.**

### Non-promises

- No pass guarantee, score guarantee, “ace the test” claim, or fixed study-time promise.
- Not the complete wording of all 409 questions in the teaching chapters and not 409 sequential explanations.
- Not a substitute for randomized practice exams, current NCVEC errata, an examination provider, or FCC rules.
- Not an operating handbook, equipment-buying guide, engineering text, or promise of real-world operating competence.
- No affiliation with or endorsement by the FCC, NCVEC, ARRL, a VEC, or a testing service.

## Why this is a paid product

Official and free sources already provide the pool, rules, practice, scoring, and video instruction. The paid layer is the combination of:

1. **Concept compression:** one reusable model replaces several isolated prompts.
2. **Original visual explanation:** diagrams show relationships that prose and flashcards make harder to retain.
3. **Misconception-first sequencing:** near-neighbor ideas are placed together and contrasted.
4. **Complete traceability:** every one of the 409 official IDs points to a teaching location, quick-reference entry, or explicit “direct recall” treatment.
5. **Physical rapid review:** an offline, large-format reference is faster to scan beside an app than a long manual or video timeline.

None of these alone justifies USD 17.99. Together they create a distinct workflow: **learn with the map, test with free software, diagnose misses through the crosswalk, revisit the relevant map.**

## Free alternative map

- **NCVEC:** the complete 2026–2030 pool, answer key, syllabus, and three official diagrams are free and public domain. Best for source truth; poor as a learning sequence.
- **FCC/eCFR:** current Part 97 is the controlling rule text. Best for legal precision; not designed as beginner exam instruction.
- **ARRL free resources:** exam review and supplementary pages support chapter review and practice. Useful, but attached to a broader instructional ecosystem.
- **HamStudy and comparable sites/apps:** excellent for spaced repetition, answer statistics, and randomized practice. They intentionally optimize individual prompts rather than a printed whole-system view.
- **YouTube/video courses:** strong for demonstration and learners who need a human explanation. Slow to rescan, inconsistent in currentness, and awkward as an ID-addressable desk reference.

The book must explicitly recommend free randomized practice. It earns payment by making those practice results more intelligible, not by withholding a free capability.

## Content architecture

The mapping counts below use the current NCVEC syllabus: T1 68, T2 37, T3 35, T4 23, T5 50, T6 46, T7 44, T8 47, T9 23, and T0 36; total 409. Chapters follow learner dependencies, not pool order.

### Front matter and navigation — 10–12 pages

- **Outcome:** understand the exam, the learn → practise → diagnose loop, notation, and how to use the crosswalk.
- **Coverage:** exam shape; pool validity; how IDs work; no substantive pool IDs consumed.
- **Visuals:** whole-book concept map, study loop, page anatomy, legend.
- **Sources:** NCVEC current pool/syllabus and errata; ARRL/VEC or official exam-structure source.

### 1. Electricity without intimidation — 18–20 pages

- **Outcome:** distinguish voltage, current, resistance, power, energy, units, prefixes, AC/DC, conductors, and insulators; solve pool-level arithmetic.
- **Mapping:** T5A–T5D (50 IDs), with cross-links to T7D measurement questions.
- **Visuals:** push/flow/opposition model; unit ladder; formula chooser; worked-pattern cards; series/parallel paths; dB direction/scale cue.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; NIST SI definitions and Ohm’s-law materials; OpenStax or equivalent authoritative/open technical reference.

### 2. Components as jobs, not symbols — 15–17 pages

- **Outcome:** identify core parts, schematic symbols, semiconductor roles, protection devices, and component functions by what they do.
- **Mapping:** T6A–T6D (46 IDs).
- **Visuals:** component family map; symbol/function pairs; one-way diode path; transistor control model; energy storage contrast; transformer and relay action.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; standard authoritative electronics references; original diagrams only.

### 3. Inside a radio and on the bench — 13–15 pages

- **Outcome:** understand signal flow through transmitter/receiver blocks, selectivity/sensitivity/modulation, and meter/test-equipment placement.
- **Mapping:** T7A and T7D; relevant T4A (about 24–28 unique IDs, exact mapping generated from parsed pool).
- **Visuals:** transmitter/receiver block flows; mixer inputs/outputs; voltmeter/ammeter/ohmmeter placement; dummy-load test path; soldering checklist.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; technical references; equipment claims constrained to pool scope.

### 4. Troubleshoot by symptom → cause → safe action — 11–13 pages

- **Outcome:** recognize overload, overdrive, RF feedback, interference, high SWR, and feed-line failure patterns without pretending to be a repair manual.
- **Mapping:** T7B–T7C (remaining T7 IDs) plus relevant T4A/T9B cross-links.
- **Visuals:** decision trees; “symptom / likely cause / first check” flows; SWR/reflected-energy picture; cable-loss path.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; FCC where interference obligations apply; technical references.

### 5. Waves, frequency, and propagation — 15–17 pages

- **Outcome:** connect frequency, wavelength, polarization, line of sight, fading, multipath, radio horizon, and named propagation modes.
- **Mapping:** T3A–T3C (35 IDs), with T9A crossover.
- **Visuals:** spectrum strip; inverse wavelength/frequency seesaw; polarization planes; line-of-sight/horizon; propagation-path mini-atlas.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; NIST/NOAA/NASA or open physics references where appropriate; no copied instructional art.

### 6. Antennas, feed lines, and SWR — 13–15 pages

- **Outcome:** distinguish common antenna shapes/behavior, gain versus power, polarization, resonant length, feed-line choices, connectors, tuners, and SWR.
- **Mapping:** T9A–T9B (23 IDs) plus relevant T3/T7 cross-links.
- **Visuals:** dipole radiation pattern; polarization match; frequency/length relationship; coax cross-section; loss stack; tuner boundary (“changes match, not antenna physics”).
- **Sources:** NCVEC; FCC band data where needed; original technical diagrams.

### 7. Signals and ways hams communicate — 13–15 pages

- **Outcome:** distinguish FM, SSB, CW, digital modes, bandwidth, satellites, Doppler, APRS/packet/DMR/WSJT, and internet linking at exam depth.
- **Mapping:** T8A–T8D (47 IDs).
- **Visuals:** modulation comparison strips; bandwidth scale; satellite uplink/downlink orbit sketch; mode-purpose map; error-correction flow.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; FCC emission/rule references; authoritative technical definitions.

### 8. First station and operating controls — 11–13 pages

- **Outcome:** assemble the station signal/power chain and know what common controls change.
- **Mapping:** T4A–T4B (23 IDs), subtracting cross-covered IDs through deterministic ownership mapping.
- **Visuals:** complete station hookup; control-to-symptom map; filters/bandwidth window; squelch/AGC/mic-gain contrasts; DMR code-plug/talk-group relationship.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; technical documentation only for generic functions.

### 9. Getting on the air: simplex, repeaters, nets, and emergencies — 13–15 pages

- **Outcome:** choose a frequency, make a contact, understand repeater offsets/tones, resolve common operating problems, and handle nets/public-service traffic within scope.
- **Mapping:** T2A–T2C (37 IDs), with linked T1/T8 items.
- **Visuals:** simplex versus repeater paths; offset input/output pair; contact flow; net hierarchy; emergency rule decision path.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; FCC Part 97 for rule claims; current band-plan facts only when clearly labeled non-FCC convention.

### 10. Rules as decision paths — 20–22 pages

- **Outcome:** decide who may transmit, where, under whose control, with what identification and content; understand license/call-sign/renewal basics and operating limits.
- **Mapping:** T1A–T1F (68 IDs).
- **Visuals:** authorization decision trees; control-operator/station relationship; identification timeline; permitted/prohibited transmission contrasts; band-edge safety margin; license lifecycle.
- **Sources:** current eCFR Part 97 controls; NCVEC identifies exam scope. Any frequency/power table is generated and double-checked from current rules.

### 11. Safety before shortcuts — 15–17 pages

- **Outcome:** recognize electrical, battery, lightning, tower, antenna, and RF-exposure hazards and choose the safe exam response.
- **Mapping:** T0A–T0C (36 IDs).
- **Visuals:** hazard hierarchy; fuse/ground/lightning paths; antenna-install stop rules; RF exposure decision flow; duty-cycle comparison.
- **Sources:** NCVEC; FCC Part 97 and OET RF-exposure materials; OSHA/NFPA concepts only if directly relevant and rights-cleared. No individualized installation advice.

### 12. Final visual review and diagnostic routes — 9–11 pages

- **Outcome:** retrieve the highest-confusion contrasts rapidly and route a missed question back to one concept page.
- **Mapping:** all groups; no new factual claims.
- **Visuals:** formula sheet; signal-flow map; rules/safety decision summary; “often confused” pairs; six short diagnostic checks.
- **Sources:** derived only from audited chapter claims and current pool.

### Complete 409-ID crosswalk, sources, glossary, index — 20–22 pages

- **Outcome:** find ID → concept/page/source status; decode essential terms; inspect provenance.
- **Mapping:** all 409 IDs exactly once as primary ownership, with optional separately labeled secondary cross-links.
- **Visuals:** compact alphabetic/numeric locator and source-key legend; not a decorative table image.
- **Sources:** frozen structured pool, claim ledger, final pagination map.

The chapter ranges leave controlled room for recto starts and final pagination while keeping the complete product within approximately 208–224 pages. The 184-page minimum is a compression fallback, not the drafting target.

## 409-question crosswalk model

The canonical pool record has one row per question with: pool version/hash, ID, subelement, group, question text hash, correct option, FCC citation if supplied, official diagram reference, errata state, and source location.

The manuscript mapping has:

- exactly one **primary concept owner** and final page anchor for every active ID;
- zero or more **secondary links**, explicitly typed and excluded from uniqueness counts;
- coverage mode: `TAUGHT`, `DIRECT_RECALL`, `CROSS_REFERENCE`, or `OFFICIAL_DIAGRAM`;
- claim IDs and visual IDs supporting the mapping;
- no answer-key reproduction unless deliberately included and independently verified.

Deterministic release checks:

1. the active source set contains exactly 409 unique IDs;
2. the 409 expected IDs equal the 409 primary mapped IDs—no missing or extra IDs;
3. every primary mapping matches its parsed subelement/group and resolves to a final anchor;
4. secondary links never count as duplicate primary coverage;
5. answer letters/values, where printed, match the frozen official key after errata;
6. official diagram references resolve; all original visual references exist and have alt text;
7. every exam-specific claim has a source; every rule citation matches refreshed eCFR;
8. source URLs, retrieval timestamps, effective dates, file hashes, parsing version, and errata set are stored;
9. pool validity is exactly 1 July 2026–30 June 2030; any withdrawal or later erratum changes the expected set and blocks stale output.

## Source hierarchy and claim ledger

1. **NCVEC February 19, 2026 public release and any later official errata/withdrawals:** controls pool wording, IDs, key, syllabus, official diagrams, validity, and public-domain statement.
2. **Current eCFR Title 47 Part 97 and FCC official publications/OET material:** controls rules, privileges, definitions, and RF exposure.
3. **NIST and other U.S. government scientific/technical sources:** controls units and technical facts when relevant.
4. **Open textbooks or university/government references with recorded licensing:** corroborate standard electronics/radio explanations.
5. **ARRL:** may corroborate exam administration/currentness and serve as market evidence, but its instructional prose and graphics are not drafting sources.
6. **Competitors, forums, videos, apps:** product/learner evidence only, never factual authority.

Each claim-ledger row includes claim ID, exact manuscript sentence or visual assertion, claim class, source URL/document, pinpoint, retrieved-at time, source hash/version, pool IDs, visual IDs, volatility, reviewer result, and final disposition. Rule/frequency/safety claims are high volatility; standard circuit relationships are low volatility. Unresolved claims are forbidden at release.

### 3. Concept graph and primary crosswalk

- **Pass:** every ID has one primary concept owner; all concepts fit the frozen architecture; no chapter becomes a disguised sequential bank.

### 4. Draft and claim ledger

- **Pass:** every exam-specific/rule/technical assertion is linked to an authoritative source and every chapter meets its reader outcome without credential claims.

### 5. Visual batch

- **Pass:** 75–95 original functional visuals, each with source claims, visual ID, grayscale proof, and alt text; no proprietary dependency.
- **Stop:** a core visual cannot be independently created or validated.

### 6. Independent factual audit

- **Pass:** a fresh pass derives answers/rules/formulas from frozen sources without relying on draft conclusions; zero unresolved critical/high claims.
- **Stop:** any safety, rule, frequency, answer-key, formula, or diagram conclusion remains disputed.

### 7. Crosswalk audit

- **Pass:** set equality across 409 official IDs and 409 unique primary mappings; all anchors and visual references resolve.
- **Stop:** any missing, extra, stale, duplicate-primary, or broken mapping remains.

### 8. Editorial and differentiation audit

- **Pass:** plain English, low jargon, one dominant idea per visual unit, no repeated answer-bank rhythm, no overpromises; full book preserves prototype experience.

### 10. Kindle conversion

### 11. Final refresh and preflight
