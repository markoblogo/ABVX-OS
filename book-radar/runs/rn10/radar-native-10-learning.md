# RN10 learning

- The previous mechanism selected books that solve explicit tasks because its entry card required a practical job and accessible unmet scenario.
- Market-first discovery immediately surfaced mainstream commercial families. It also exposed a different failure mode: category demand can be real while entry for a new title remains unproved.
- Commercial tier must precede production effort. Otherwise short generic nonfiction will outrank harder fiction despite weaker demand.
- Fiction is not “easy text.” Editing, cover, reader satisfaction, series cadence and discoverability belong in its production estimate.
- Category growth plus a reader request is insufficient. Finalists need multiple purchase or traction origins and a concrete discovery path.
- The next gate should compare premises and positioning inside the two retained markets. It should not draft both books.
- A post-run assessment admitted `last30days-skill` as an optional nomination sensor for future runs. Its recent cross-source engagement and Amazon review lane can improve topic discovery and reader-language capture, but none of those outputs bypass the purchase, reachability or economics gates.
- A user-supplied fiction premise can enter after a run without being misreported as a Radar discovery. Give it a separate concept gate and preserve the original scan counts and finalists.
- RN10-003 shows that tonal contrast can be the hook: a genuinely cozy dog-owner opening makes the later black-comic system apocalypse sharper. Listing metadata must reveal the destination while the cover preserves the inviting first read.
