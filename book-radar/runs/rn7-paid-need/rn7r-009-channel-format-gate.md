# RN7R-009 channel and format gate

Checked: 2026-09-08  
Scope: public evidence only; no product, listing, advertisement, outreach, payment, or buyer data was created or collected.

## Decision

**CONSIDER_DIGITAL_FORMAT — separate portfolio decision required.**

The buyer problem remains credible, and a self-service tool can add value beyond a generic checklist. The strongest use format is an immediately available, reusable digital packet that several family members can update. That is not the current KDP-first product model. Amazon is reachable without an existing audience, but paperback delivery and fixed write-in space fit a decision measured in days less well. Etsy contains paid family-care printables, yet the exact agency-comparison evidence is weak and price expectations are low. Gumroad can deliver the product but does not give a new seller usable discovery until prior genuine sales establish eligibility.

The gate therefore does not authorize production. It also does not erase the earlier `READY_FOR_VALIDATION` decision: that remains the historical result of RN7 paid-need discovery.

## Buyer and buying moment

- **Decision maker / payer:** most plausibly an adult child, spouse, or other family member arranging privately paid non-medical home care. The person receiving care may participate, but the family member doing the calls and comparing proposals performs the job and is the likely buyer.
- **Trigger:** after a need assessment or consultation produces two or more agency proposals, typically while the family is trying to start help within days.
- **Search path:** the buyer first searches for how to hire or compare care, reaches free guidance or agency directories, then may search Amazon or Etsy for a checklist, guide, workbook, or printable. This path is plausible but not measured as a conversion funnel.
- **Important boundary:** evidence of families hiring care proves the decision exists. It does not prove that those families buy a separate comparison tool.

Current AARP guidance confirms the decision structure: families assess needs, choose between agency and direct hire, arrange consultations, ask written questions, and compare issues such as minimum hours, backup coverage, responsibility, and cost. It also supplies free guidance, so a paid product cannot merely restate questions.

## Channel check

### 1. Amazon KDP

**Path:** urgent family need → Amazon search for a caregiver hiring guide/workbook → paperback product page → order → manual completion.

**Evidence:** the exact stored query showed only one directly relevant 1995 family guide with one rating, while adjacent caregiving books and logs had visible paid traction. Current KDP permits print-on-demand with no inventory. At a hypothetical $14.99 list price, a 60-page black-ink 7.5 × 9.25 paperback on Amazon.com would use the 60% tier and the current $2.84 large-trim printing cost, leaving about **$6.15 per sale before advertising and fixed production costs**.

**Access for a new author:** technically open and does not require an existing audience. Organic discovery for the narrow query is unproven; no current conversion, traffic, BSR, or keyword-volume evidence is available.

**Constraints:** physical delivery is slower than the buyer's decision; a single copy is awkward for remote family collaboration; repeated comparisons consume writing space. Advertising may be needed, but current CAC is unknown. Amazon impressions would not show whether viewers are actively holding multiple agency proposals.

**Conclusion:** valid KDP channel, but only a partial format fit and not a reliable source of ten qualified exposures on demand.

### 2. Etsy digital downloads

**Path:** urgent family need → Etsy/Google search for a caregiver printable or hiring guide → instant digital download → fill digitally or print copies → share with family.

**Evidence:** Etsy currently shows high-review caregiver logs and planners, family hiring guides priced about $7.99–$39.95, and one directly adjacent “private vs. agency” comparison printable at $2.99 with one review. This proves that buyers purchase caregiving printables, but not that a $12 proposal normalizer has demand. The shelf is crowded with low-priced generic logs.

**Access for a new author:** a new French seller can open a shop and list a digital item, subject to onboarding. Current base fees include a $0.20 listing/renewal fee, 6.5% transaction fee, France payment processing of 4% + €0.30, and a 1.14% French regulatory operating fee; VAT on seller fees and optional advertising can add cost. At **€12**, the contribution is approximately **€10.10 before VAT on fees, ads, refunds, support, and fixed costs**.

**Constraints:** a new listing competes with established, discounted printables; organic placement and qualified traffic are unknown. Etsy Ads can buy visibility but not necessarily reach people currently comparing proposals. Shop setup, customer support, and file/version management expand the portfolio beyond KDP.

**Conclusion:** best match to the use case, but commercial access is not yet demonstrated and adopting it requires a separate business-model decision.

### 3. Gumroad / direct link

**Path:** buyer encounters an external link in search, content, a referral, or an owned audience → Gumroad checkout → instant packet.

**Evidence:** Gumroad supports instant digital products. Direct-link sales currently cost 10% + $0.50 plus payment processing; marketplace discovery sales cost 30%. More importantly, Discover eligibility requires at least $100 in genuine prior sales plus risk review and at least one product sale.

**Access for a new author:** checkout is accessible; the buyer is not. Without an existing audience, search traffic, partnerships, ads, or manual outreach, Gumroad is infrastructure rather than an acquisition channel.

**Constraints:** reaching the first ten qualified buyers would require precisely the ongoing recruitment or external traffic that this gate is meant to avoid.

**Conclusion:** reject as the validation channel for the present experiment.

## Can ten qualified exposures be obtained within the attention budget?

**Not demonstrated.** Amazon and Etsy may generate impressions with low ongoing labor, but neither exposes whether viewers currently have two or more agency proposals. Paid ads could buy clicks, not verified qualification, and CAC is unavailable. Caregiver communities, agencies, employer programs, senior centers, and referral partners could improve qualification, but access requires manual recruitment or partnership permission and was not assumed.

The earlier estimate of about 30 minutes of Anton time covered packaging approval only; it did not include channel setup or participant acquisition. A defensible qualified-buyer test through direct recruitment would likely exceed that bound, and public evidence does not support a narrower estimate.

## Format comparison

### A. KDP paperback/workbook

- Good for handwritten notes and a durable family record.
- Weak on immediate access, reuse across more than a few proposals, remote collaboration, and later updates.
- Natural channel: Amazon. Author support can remain near zero.
- Portfolio fit: **strong**. Buyer-use fit: **partial**.
- Do not pad a four-page tool into a book. It would need a genuinely useful self-service reference layer, not extra pages for spine width.

### B. Standalone digital comparison packet

- Best for immediate access, duplicateable proposal pages, typed updates, printing, and family sharing.
- Natural channel: Etsy; Gumroad only when traffic already exists.
- Support can be low if the file is self-explanatory and does not promise document review, but compatibility questions and version management remain.
- Portfolio fit: **not established**. This is a diagnostic alternative, not authorization to expand the business model.

### C. No product under the current model

- Avoids forcing a digitally native urgent task into a paperback.
- Preserves Anton's attention and avoids an unvalidated acquisition channel.
- Sacrifices the chance to test willingness to pay until a low-touch qualified channel is identified or a digital-product exception is approved.

## Paid advantage over free materials

**Input:** two to four actual agency proposals, consultation notes, and written follow-ups entered by the buyer.

**Transformation:** the self-service system maps each offer into the same fields—schedule and minimum hours, included duties, price and extra fees, start/cancellation terms, backup process, supervision, exclusions, assumptions, and missing answers—then converts blank or inconsistent fields into agency-specific written questions.

**Output:** one comparable decision record, an unresolved-questions list for each agency, an answer log, and the family's stated reason for its choice.

This exceeds a free checklist because it transforms inconsistent proposals into a traceable comparison rather than merely suggesting questions. The value survives without personal processing if instructions and examples are sufficient. It must not score clinical quality, certify safety, interpret law or contracts, or recommend an agency. If buyers demand those judgments, the product fails the autonomous low-support model.

## Economics and the next test

The evidence supports **demand for the underlying care decision**, but not willingness to pay for this tool, demand for either final format, or economical acquisition.

- **Paperback scenario:** $14.99 list; estimated KDP royalty $6.15 at 60 pages, large trim, black ink; shipping is paid by the buyer/Amazon terms; CAC unknown; expected author support near zero. This is a scenario, not a forecast or final specification.
- **Digital scenario:** €12 Etsy price; approximately €10.10 contribution before VAT on fees, optional ads, refunds, support, and fixed costs; CAC unknown. Currency choice, setup fee, and final tax treatment remain account-specific.

The only useful next test is **channel qualification**, not another product sample: determine whether one low-touch channel can deliver ten people currently holding at least two agency proposals. Continue only if the source, qualification method, expected cost, and Anton workload can be specified before launch. Stop if access requires recurring manual outreach, professional partnerships, or paid acquisition with no bounded CAC. An inconclusive result is fewer than ten verified qualified exposures; it is not a rejection of the market.

This test would measure access to current buyers. It would not prove KDP demand, long-run conversion, organic ranking, or unit economics. The old “2 of 10” payment threshold is not retained because the acquisition population is not yet established and ten observations would remain directional, not statistically reliable.
