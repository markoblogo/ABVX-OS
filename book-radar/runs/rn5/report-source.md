## Direct answer

RN5-001, Solar Quote Decoder, survives as a conditional production candidate. A representative product is required because the paid value depends on worksheet hierarchy and writing ergonomics. The eight-page sample exists; full production is blocked pending the human product gate.

- The U.S. Department of Energy advises solar shoppers to compare installer quotes and normalize price as cost per watt: https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/articles/decisions-decisions-choosing-solar-installer
- The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau describes hidden solar-loan markups and recommends obtaining written breakdowns and comparing multiple installer and lender proposals: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-advisory-steer-clear-of-costly-and-complex-loans-for-solar-energy-installation/
- The Federal Trade Commission treats deceptive solar sales as a current consumer problem and advises obtaining reputable quotes: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/09/solar-energy-rising-popularity-so-are-scams
- EnergySage reports that its 2025 marketplace analysis covers millions of transaction-level observations from U.S. homeowners shopping for solar and storage. It is commercially interested evidence, used here only to establish active comparison behavior: https://www.energysage.com/press/energysage-marketplace-intel-report-21/
- Current Amazon observation: the exact `solar quote comparison workbook` search returned one tangential result. The broader homeowner solar shelf returned 342 results; a May 2026 guide showed 17 ratings and a June 2026 guide showed 167. These are visible shelf signals, not verified unit sales: https://www.amazon.com/s?k=solar+quote+comparison+workbook&i=stripbooks and https://www.amazon.com/s?k=solar+panel+buying+guide+homeowner&i=stripbooks

## Contradiction and uncertainty

Strong free alternatives exist, including official guidance and marketplace comparison tools. The product can win only if vendor-neutral ownership of the buyer's actual quotes and the paper decision packet are materially better than those alternatives. Exact-workbook newcomer traction was not observed, BSR was unavailable, and 90-day economics are hypotheses. A failed human product gate or a direct-competitor rescan showing meaningful traction kills the candidate.

## Stop rationale

The top-ranked candidate has sufficient official problem evidence, an observable exact shelf gap, adjacent newcomer evidence, a low-dependency source model and a bounded sample gate. More broad search would not resolve the remaining product-experience uncertainty; the correct next evidence is human review of the representative sample.
