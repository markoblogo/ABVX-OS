## Rights gate

This pass applies a conservative U.S. publishing screen. The U.S. Copyright Office states that works published in the United States before 1 January 1931 are public domain; a foreign original and any particular translation, illustrations, notes, or modern edition must be checked independently. Candidate production must use a named, traceable public-domain source file, and new translation/typography only. Project Gutenberg's record for *Jekyll and Hyde* expressly labels the supplied English text public domain in the USA.

## Top 3 deep dive

### 1. *The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* — winner

- **Source / rights:** Robert Louis Stevenson, 1886; Project Gutenberg eBook 42, explicitly marked public domain in the USA. Use that canonical plain-text/HTML record, not a modern cover, introduction or adaptation.
- **Length / form:** compact English novella, nine titled sections; economical enough for a complete parallel English / Toki Pona / sitelen edition.
- **Market evidence:** Project Gutenberg reports 39,063 downloads in the last 30 days in its current item record. This is not Amazon sales, but it is stronger current public reading interest than a purely inferred classic choice.
- **Toki Pona competition:** targeted web/Amazon search in this pass found no discoverable complete Toki Pona edition; this is a low-to-medium-confidence absence, not proof that none exists. A pre-production gate must repeat Amazon/lipu-sona/community searches.
- **Buyer / value:** Toki Pona readers, conlang readers, collectors of transformed classics and readers attracted by a concise famous Gothic story. The transformation clarifies recurring person/identity/action anchors while sitelen makes duality and the story's object vocabulary visible.
- **Format:** paperback primary, fixed-layout Kindle secondary; a three-layer layout and identity/name guide turn it into an owned object rather than a bare translation.
- **Automation value:** controlled entity ledger (Jekyll/Hyde/Utterson), chapter-level terminology consistency, parallel-text layout and sitelen rendering are a strong next pipeline test.
- **Strongest reason to produce:** recognisable, public-domain, compact, narratively different from the existing philosophy line, and pipeline-friendly.
- **Strongest reason not to:** incorrectly simplifying its tone or withholding/revealing information can make the reading edition feel shallow.

### 2. *The Time Machine*

- **Source / rights:** H. G. Wells, 1895 English original; Project Gutenberg supplies the complete text. Use a named public-domain source; exclude modern annotations/art.
- **Length / form:** 16 sections plus epilogue, a manageable novella.
- **Market evidence:** canonical early SF and a current Project Gutenberg full text; no reliable Amazon sales number captured in this pass.
- **Competition:** no complete TP edition found in the limited scoped search; low confidence pending a real pre-production search.
- **Value / format:** visual chapter maps, time-scale anchors and a restrained terminology guide could make sitelen material rather than decorative.
- **Risk:** “time”, class, evolution and scientific speculation demand careful semantic notes; an over-literal compression risks losing the intellectual point.
- **Why it loses:** higher terminology and conceptual QA burden than Jekyll, with no stronger observed catalogue fit.

### 3. *The Importance of Being Earnest*

- **Source / rights:** Oscar Wilde, first performed/published 1895; English source requires final source-specific rights verification before production.
- **Length / form:** short play, likely low layout cost and good speech-turn automation test.
- **Market / competition:** highly recognisable in literature culture, but no stronger direct-book-demand evidence captured than its literary status; no complete TP edition surfaced in limited search.
- **Value:** can demonstrate how Toki Pona handles names, social claims and dialogue; sitelen could support speaker navigation rather than decorate every line.
- **Risk:** the product's core—wit, wordplay, social register—is exactly what radical lexical reduction can destroy.
- **Why it loses:** high artistic translation risk relative to commercial/pipeline information value.

## Selected next candidate

**Recommend: _The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde — Toki Pona Edition with Sitelen Pona_.** It dominates the next experiment on information value + likely object value + low production cost: a famous, complete, compact and visual English public-domain narrative outside the existing Stoic/Chinese/classic-epic cluster. It also tests a reusable narrative-entity / parallel-layout pipeline without betting on a long text.

**Rights confidence: high for the named Project Gutenberg U.S. source; conditional for every other market and asset.** The pre-production gate must verify distribution territories, source checksum/provenance, title/author treatment and that no supplied edition material is copied.

**Estimated production complexity: medium.** The translation itself is not a mechanical operation; it needs chapter-level semantic QA and human literary review. Layout/rendering is low-to-medium because it can reuse Odyssey-style parallel layers.
